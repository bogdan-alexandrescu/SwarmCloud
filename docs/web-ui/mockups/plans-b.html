Plans: proposal B, Plan as a review page

One of four competing proposals for BGD's redesign of how the console shows what automatic runs propose. The four share Config E's and the Automate redesign's style (#2417) and differ in layout and flow. Every frame has the same id in every proposal (P1 to P7). The theme control at the top repaints the page and every frame, and so does the toggle in each frame's panel foot.

Idea

A plan is a full page made for deciding, laid out like a pull request review. A sticky head carries the source (schedule, firing, issue) and the decision: Approve, Edit, Reject with a reason. Below it the evidence reads top to bottom in the order a reviewer judges: summary, requirements as a checklist, overlaps with work in flight, then the steps as cards, each with its files, tests, estimate, runner and model, its prompt folded and its risk paths called out. A rail beside it holds why the plan exists, its cost and time, the risk check, and its digest and revision. Each section has a "read" tick, like a review's "viewed", so a long plan shows how much of it you have read. The landing, Automate › Approvals, lists every proposal from every source and opens this page.

Pros

  • Depth: nothing about the plan is behind a second click except the prompt, and the prompt is one fold away.
  • The page approves exactly what it shows: the button names the digest and revision, and a newer revision turns it off.
  • One address per plan (/automate/approvals/run:run_…), the same page from the inbox, from Work › Runs and from the issue comment's link.

Risks

  • It is long. A one-step plan with five overlaps is a tall page, and a quick "approve" is a scroll away from most evidence (the sticky head keeps the button in reach, which also makes approving unread easy; the read ticks only count, they do not gate).
  • Runner, model, a money estimate and per-source counts are not served today (see New fields).

How it differs from the console today

  • Today a PLANNED plan shows in Work › Runs' detail pane (Runs.tsx PlanBody): summary lead, steps, a folded full plan, and the approve, edit, reject buttons under it. Here it is a page of its own with the decision pinned on top.
  • Today Automate › Approvals projects the run as kind plan or hold beside merges and proposals, as a list and a split pane. Here Approvals is the landing for every proposal, with source and state filters, and a plan row opens the review page.
  • Today the editor is a form over the whole plan. Here editing happens in place on the same page, step by step, and ends on a diff against the revision shown.

P3

P3Editing a plan

Edit turns the same page editable in place. Saving sends the whole plan with the digest it replaces (plan:edit, D3), through the planner's own validator; the server answers the new digest and bumps the revision.

P3aEditing: change the step, edit its prompt, add a requirement

The only step cannot be removed: a plan needs at least one step, and the button says so. The mode and the overlaps are the planner's findings and are not editable, as today.

P3bRemoving a step (example: a two-step plan)

A removed step stays drawn, struck through, until you save, and Undo brings it back. Today’s editor has no remove: this is new UI over the same plan:edit, which takes the whole plan. A step another step depends_on would say so before it could be removed.

P3cSaved: the new revision, its digest, and approve the edited plan

The head shows revision 1 by you, its digest as the save answered it, and a diff against revision 0. Approve carries the new digest. The edit changed step 1 and the requirements, so it unticked both and the head reads 2 of 4; Edit stays, for a revision 2. Under a second_member hold, the last plan editor may not approve (a workspace admin may), and the head would say so.

Fields

New fields this proposal needs

Every other figure is served today by apps/swarm-api (routes/runs.py, routes/approvals.py, routes/schedules.py) or derived from served fields, as each caption says.

1 · plan.steps[].runner: the runner profile and model a step will run on

What: {runner_profile, model} per step, resolved when the plan is made from the schedule's run_options or the run's defaults. Why: P2 draws "runner and model" on each step card. Today a step carries title, prompt, files, tests, estimate, depends_on only, so the card draws a dash with "not served". Invariant 10 holds: it is read-only, resolved by the platform, never taken from a caller.

2 · plan.cost_estimate: an expected spend range in dollars

What: {low_usd, high_usd, basis}, computed by the API from the steps' estimates and the runner's rates, not written by the planner. Why: P2's rail draws the expected cost. Today only the free-text estimate exists ("2 agent-hours"), so the cost is a dash with its reason.

3 · hold_check on a PLANNED run: which stop categories were checked

What: the categories hard_stop_hold checked for this repository (IAM, workflows, the frozen contract, secrets, tenancy, shared, cost) and whether the repository is platform: true. Why: P2's risk card says "checked, none matched" per category. Today a null approval_hold says nothing matched, but not which checks ran; without the field the card says only "no hold".

4 · proposed_7d and approved_7d on a schedule

What: counts of plans or proposals the schedule's firings produced in seven days, and how many were approved (by a person or its gate). Why: P5's two columns. Today they would need every firing's work read.

Not new: the newer revision on a stale read (P2c)

What: when the page holds digest A and the run now serves digest B, the API already serves B with plan_revision and plan_edited_by; P2c's "what changed" is a diff the browser computes between the plan it holds and the plan served. Listed so nobody builds a field for it.

Not new: the "read" ticks and the required reason

The per-section "read" ticks and the head's "read 3 of 4" are kept in the browser for the plan revision on screen; an edit unticks what it changed. They are a reading aid, not a gate, and nothing is sent. The reject reason is optional in plan:reject; this page requires it before it sends one.

Not new: the landing

P1 is GET /v1/approvals (records and projected runs, with kind, subject.schedule_id, subject.firing_id, state, expires_at, may_decide). A run made by hand has expires_at: null and its row says "no expiry". The source chip is derived from the schedule's type, or "by hand" without one.
