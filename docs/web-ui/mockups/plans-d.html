Plans, proposal D: Plan conversation

One of four competing proposals (A to D) for how the console shows what automatic runs propose and how a plan is judged before it is approved. Each draws the same frames (P1 to P7). This one treats a plan as a reviewed document with a thread: the planner's reasons sit beside each step, you comment on a step or ask for changes, the planner answers with a new revision (its digest, and a diff from the last), and Approve applies to the revision on the screen.

Idea

Today a plan can be accepted, edited by hand or rejected. Here it can also be shaped: a comment on a step, a requirement or an overlap goes to the planner, which answers in the thread with a new revision. Every revision keeps its digest and its diff from the one before, so "what am I approving" is always one revision, drawn whole, with how it got there beside it. Everything automatic runs propose lands in Automate › Approvals as threads, the ones waiting on you first; a plan opens as a decision page (the #2417 A5 shape), the document on the left and its margin (reasons and thread) on the right. After approval the same thread carries on: steps to tasks, the pull request, CI rounds, the review, the merge.

How it differs from the console today

  • Today the plan is in Work › Runs (Runs.tsx) and the inbox projects it as kind plan. Here the plan's page lives at /automate/approvals/<id> and Runs links to it.
  • Today there is one plan and a revision number (plan_revision, plan_edited_by). Here every revision is kept and readable, with its diff.
  • Today the only way to change a plan is to edit it yourself. Here you can also ask the planner, and its answer is a revision you approve or edit.
  • Today the plan's banner says anyone in the tenant may approve, edit or reject (Runs.tsx), and the server refuses one the schedule's approvers exclude. Here the page says who can decide, and anyone may comment.

Pros and risks

  • Pro: a plan that is nearly right is fixed in one comment instead of a reject and a fresh plan.
  • Pro: the reason for each step is on the page, so judging it needs no trip to the issue comment.
  • Risk: every answer is a planner run, so a conversation costs agent time; each one is shown with its cost.
  • Risk: needs the most new API of the four: revisions, a thread, and a re-plan on request (listed at the end).
  • Risk: a long thread can hide the plan itself; the margin folds resolved comments.

P1

Where proposals land: Automate › Approvals, as threads

Every proposal is a thread: what it is, where it came from, its state, and the last word in it. Waiting on a person first, oldest first, with time to expiry; then everything decided. The pane on the right is the selected thread's latest revision and last messages, so a glance tells you whether it needs reading.

P2

One plan to decide: run_fba2dc8f6ec9494dae2f

The decision page. The plan is the document on the left, in the order you judge it: why it exists, summary, requirements, overlaps, steps, risk, estimate and cost. The margin on the right holds, against each part, the planner's reason and the thread. The decision bar names the revision and digest it approves.

P3

Editing a plan: ask the planner, or edit it yourself

Two ways to shape a plan, and both end in a revision with its own digest. In this example the platform owner (Operator, the viewer) comments on the step, the planner answers with revision 2, and the owner then edits revision 2 by hand into revision 3 and approves it. Every revision after 1, every comment and every digest after the first is example data.

P4

After approval: the thread carries on

Approval does not close the page; the same thread follows the plan to what it became. Each step's task, the pull request, each CI round and fix round, the review's verdict against each requirement, the merge, and the issue line. The outcomes below are examples.

P5

The automatic sources and their gates

Automate › Schedules, narrowed to the schedules that propose: what each proposes, its gates as stored (gate.run, gate.plan, gate.merge, gate.approvers, gate.approval_ttl_hours), its spend today, and how much it proposed and how much was approved. A row opens the schedule's sheet; the merge column's switch is the audited one.
