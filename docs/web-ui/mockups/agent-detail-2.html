2Variant A: tabs and cards in the split detail

Variant A

Children get a tab, the verdict and merge get cards, and the Code card starts with what happened

Recommended

The agent detail gets one new tab, Children, shown only when the agent has submitted any or the API cannot say. A child carries a link back to its parent above its title. On the workflow page the review step shows its verdict as a card, and the merge step shows a list of its checks. The Code card always opens with one sentence about the outcome and what to do, then the base pin, then the approved per-file diff.

A2 · A child cancelled by its parent

A3 · The review verdict, post-verdict, and a refused merge

A3b · Every check passes, and the human gate holds the merge

A4 · Publish outcomes in the Code card

Collapsed list and phone

Idea

Keep the approved split detail and add to it: one tab (Children), one link (back to the parent), two cards on the workflow page (verdict, merge checks), and an outcome sentence at the top of the Code card. No new screens and no new routes beyond /agents/children/<id>.

Pros

  • Each fact sits next to the object it describes, as on the rest of the console.
  • Children never appear as DAG nodes, which matches the design: children are not steps, and the workflow rollup does not count them.
  • The six causes and the new FAILED forms share one headline pattern: what happened, then what to do.

Risks

  • A tab is easy to miss. The parked callout links to it, and the tab only shows when there are children.
  • "Findings by severity" depends on a field nothing writes yet (see section 1).
  • The human-gate shape is assumed until B13r lands.
Alternatives
Variant B

The family lives in the list, as a tree

The agent list nests each parent's children under it as indented rows that open and close. The workflow page shows a row of small dots under any step that has children. Verdict and merge are the same as in A.

Idea

You see the family at a glance in the list. Opening a child is one click away from its siblings.

Pros

  • Easy to see which children are still running, without opening the parent.
  • On the workflow page the dots show that a step fanned out.

Risks

  • The list is sorted and paged by state and time. A child can sort apart from its parent, or land on another page, so the tree needs a second query per parent.
  • An opened parent with 16 children pushes the rest of the list off screen.
  • The workflow dots come close to the "tree from depends_on" the design forbids.
Variant C

The workflow page becomes the hub: a "path to merge" rail

The workflow page gets a rail on the right: review verdict, posted review, fix, proof, merge checks, and the human gate, as one vertical path that ends at "ready". The agent detail keeps only the outcome sentence and links to the rail. Children get a "Helpers" section on the workflow page under the step that submitted them.

Idea

Merging is about the workflow, not one step, so put the whole path in one place.

Pros

  • "Why hasn't it merged?" is answered in one place.
  • Good fit for the single-pr chain, which always has the same six steps.

Risks

  • Useless for a workflow that is not single-pr, or a lone agent. Those would need a second design.
  • Children only exist on workflow steps here, but most parents will be lone agents.
  • It shows the merge checks twice: on the rail and in the step.

3Recommendation

Variant A, with C's merge checklist kept as the merge step's own card

A adds to the screens already approved and draws nothing the design rules out. Children appear only through parent_task_id and never as DAG edges. B's tree fights the list's sorting and paging. C works only for single-pr workflows, and parents are usually lone agents. The one good thing in C, a single ordered list of what stands between a PR and its merge, is kept in A as the merge step's checklist.

Small choices made in the frames

  1. Children tab visibility: shown when the agent has children, or when the API cannot say (with a dash). Hidden when it reports zero.
  2. Children order: unfinished first (running, then queued), then failed, then succeeded. Each group is in submission order.
  3. "Waiting on N": counts the children that have not ended. A failed or cancelled child counts as ended, because the scheduler promotes on terminal states, not on success.
  4. Fan-out slots: drawn as 16 small blocks. The cap comes from the API, so a tenant with another cap draws another number.
  5. Await deadline: shown as a time and as "in 23h". Await refunds are shown as "1 of 4 used".
  6. Severity: Blocker, Major, Minor, then Not graded. A finding without a severity is never treated as a blocker.
  7. Merge checklist: listed in the design's row order. Checks after the first refusal show a dash ("not read"), not a cross.
  8. Human gate: worded "awaiting a person: add ready". The console never adds the label.
  9. Credential refusals: the file is listed in red with its content withheld. The value is never shown, not even redacted.
  10. Base pin: one line under the outcome. "Pinned" names the upstream step and its commit, and "not pinned" gives the reason. Steps that build on nothing say "does not apply".

·What the code does not say yet

  • published_nothing, base_pin, merge_human_gate and awaiting_human are not on main, on the recent branches, or in issues. Their shapes here are my reading of the brief.
  • The verdict schema has no severity. Grouping by severity needs a field nothing writes today.
  • The human gate is drawn as "the step checks everything and does not merge, and a person adds ready". If B13r means the step adds the label itself, A3b's two frames merge into one.
  • The merge step and post-verdict are an accepted design, not built. Their build waits on #342.
