Automate: pick a design

Four complete proposals for the console’s Automate section, each drawn in the Config E and Subscription accounts style. Every proposal draws the same seven frames, A1 to A7, from the same kind of example data, so any frame can be taken from any proposal. For each frame, pick one proposal or none of these, add a note if you want, then press Copy my picks and paste the text into #2417.

Under each frame, the tabs switch between the four proposals’ versions. The preview is that proposal’s own frame, live and scaled down; it shows the top of the frame. To read a frame, open it at full size: the link opens the proposal at that frame. Every figure in the proposals is an example. Your picks and notes are kept in this browser for convenience; the copied text is the record.

·The four proposals

Each proposal’s idea, and the fields it needs that the API does not serve today, as its own “New fields” list names them. Every other figure it draws is served by apps/swarm-api today or derived from served fields.

A

List and side sheet

Automate keeps its two tabs, Schedules and Approvals. Each opens on a one-sentence lead that names what needs you, then one dense table. A schedule or an approval opens in a right side sheet over its list, as an account does on Subscription accounts, so the next row is one click away. Creating or editing a schedule is the same sheet in six steps, each showing the refusal the server would raise there. The State column shows six words that say what to do: active, paused, paused by its tripwire, waiting on approval, failing, never fired.

New fields it needs (5)

  • approval.may_decide {approve, reject, why}
  • approval.superseded_by {approval_id, head_sha, at}
  • firing.work[].pull_request {number, state}
  • firing.largest_run_usd
  • schedules.summary on GET /v1/schedules {by_state, pending_approvals, oldest_expires_at}

B

One inbox, decision first

Automate opens on Waiting on you: one list of everything that needs a person (approvals to decide, schedules their spend tripwire paused, schedules that are failing), most urgent first. The schedules follow underneath. Every waiting row opens a decision page in one order: the question in one sentence, the evidence in numbered cards, then Approve or Deny with a reason in a bar pinned under the evidence. A schedule’s page is Config E cards instead of five tabs. The two tabs stand.

New fields it needs (4)

  • merge.checks {passed, failed, pending} at the head, on a merge approval
  • work[].pull_request on a firing’s issue-run work item
  • superseded_by and head_sha_now on a superseded approval
  • reason_files on a hold: which matched file each reason came from

C

Time first

Automate is read against time. The landing is a swimlane, one lane per schedule on one shared axis: the last 7 days on the left, the next 24 hours magnified on the right of a “now” line. Past firings are dots coloured by outcome, next firings are rings, and a band under each lane shows each day’s spend against its cap. An approval that blocks a firing is pinned where it blocks. One approval shows where it sits in its schedule’s timeline and what each answer does to the next firing.

New fields it needs (10)

  • spend_days[] on each listed schedule, for the last 7 days
  • firings_since on GET /v1/schedules: each schedule’s firings over the window
  • next_runs[] on each schedule: the next firings within 24 hours
  • work[].pull_request {number, state, head_sha} on a firing’s work item
  • blocks on an approval: the firing it holds and its effect on the next firing
  • effect_on_decide on an approval’s detail: what approve, reject and expiry each do
  • superseded_by and superseded_why on an approval
  • merged {head_sha, at} on an approved merge’s detail
  • owner_name on an approval whose approvers are owner only
  • budget_use on the list route

D

Config E sections

Automate is drawn the way Config E draws Config, and led by what it is waiting for. A setup strip runs across the top (schedule types enabled, approvers set, budget tripwires set, merge App set), each step read, never ticked by hand. Under it come fixed sections: Needs you (approvals waiting and schedules stuck), schedules as cards grouped by type, then today’s firings. Detail pages open with a lead that carries the question and its answers, then stack their cards.

New fields it needs (7)

  • merge_app {state, checked_at} on a new GET /v1/automate/setup
  • types {available, total, unavailable[]} on the same read
  • approval.may_decide {allowed, why, deciders[]}
  • approval.superseded {head_sha, at, superseded_by}
  • firing.failure {code, detail}, and on last_firing
  • policy.max_consecutive_failures, served and writable
  • GET /v1/firings?since=&limit=

A1Automate’s landing

Schedules in every state (active, paused, paused by its spend tripwire, waiting on an approval, failing, never fired) and the approvals waiting.

Schedules is a dense table, one row per schedule with its six-word state, under a one-sentence lead that names what needs you; the approvals waiting sit in a card below.

Waiting on you comes first: one list, most urgent first, of approvals to decide and schedules stopped by a tripwire or failing. The schedules table follows.

A swimlane per schedule on one time axis: seven days of firings as dots, the next 24 hours as rings, and the approval that holds a firing pinned on its lane.

A setup strip, then Needs you (approvals waiting, schedules stuck), then schedules as cards grouped by type, then today’s firings.

A2One schedule

What it does, its type and target, next firings, its firing history and outcomes, spend against its tripwire, its gates and approvers, and its actions.

A side sheet over the list, read top to bottom: what it does, cadence and next firings, the firings with their pull requests, spend today, gates and approvers.

A page of cards behind a lead that says what this schedule is waiting on: a fact strip, what it does, cadence and next firings, spend against the tripwire with per-run bars, gates, then the firing history.

The schedule’s own lane drawn tall beside the list: firings, cost and spend over 7 days and the next 24 hours, then the firing history and settings.

A lead with the one thing it waits on, then cards: what it does, when it fires, spend against the tripwire with five run bars, gates and approvers, firing history.

A3A new or edited schedule

Type and template, target, cadence, gates, budget, validation, and a preview of the next firings.

A side sheet in six steps (type, target, cadence, gates, budget, review); each step checks what the server checks there, so a refusal shows on the step that caused it.

One page of numbered cards with a side sheet that keeps the server’s refusals and the next five firings in view; an edit shows the change against the current revision.

The editor opens beside the list, and its cadence preview is a lane among the schedules already set; each refusal sits next to its field and is summed at the top.

Config E’s wizard: one side sheet, six steps, each drawn on its own; the review lists only what changed, and an edit that lost a race says so with the way out.

A4Approvals

Waiting (oldest first, with expiry), decided (by whom), and expired, filtered by kind.

The second tab, full width: waiting oldest first with time to expiry and who may decide, then decided with who, when and the reason, then expired and superseded; the kind filter is the API’s.

The record: waiting oldest first, decided (approved, denied or superseded) with who, when and the reason, then expired; a kind filter with every kind’s count.

Opens on time: each waiting approval is a bar from its request to its expiry against a “now” line; below, the list grouped waiting, decided and ended without a decision, filtered by kind.

An inbox: waiting oldest first with time left and who may decide, decided in the last seven days, then expired and superseded, with the built kind filter and its counts.

Where it sits in its schedule’s timeline (the firing that asked, the firings it holds up, its expiry), then what Approve, Deny and no decision each do to the next firing.

A6Empty and honest states

No schedules (A6a); Automate not served by the deployment (A6b); an unreadable read, a dash with its reason and never 0 (A6c); nothing waiting (A6d).

What the tabs say in each case: a count that was not read is a dash with its reason, and a 0 is drawn only when the list was read and is empty.

The inbox carries the state: type tiles to start the first schedule, a dash with the failed read’s reason, and 0 only when nothing waits.

Each state is the whole screen; the time axis stays when there is no lane, so the page reads as the same page with nothing on it, not as an error.

The setup strip leads a new workspace; not served takes over the content area with the nav still usable; a failed read is a dash with its reason in the panel and in Needs you.
