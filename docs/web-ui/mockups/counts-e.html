EPeriod comparison

Idea

Every figure is shown against the period just before it, bucket against bucket: week 1 of these 26 weeks against week 1 of the 26 before. The section opens with one sentence that says what changed and what drove it. Four charts follow (agents with retries apart, run hours, tokens, estimated cost), each with this period's line over a ghosted dashed line of the period before. Top movers names the agent type, model, workspace or pool account that grew or shrank most. Share of total sets each group's slice this period beside its slice last time. Picking a breakdown turns the four charts into one small chart per group, each over its own ghost. The live state counts stay, below the charts, and one priced Run reads both.

Pros

  • Answers the question a counts page is opened for, "is it growing, and what is driving it", in one read and one sentence.
  • A change is a direction glyph (▲ ▼) and a number, never green or red: more cost is not good or bad on its own, and the glyph survives greyscale.
  • Honest by construction: a change is shown only between two like figures (closed weeks against closed weeks, complete against complete). Anything else withholds the change and says why.
  • Movers are ranked by the change itself, not the percent, so a group that went from 3 to 9 does not lead the list.
  • Builds on what the Timeline already has: compare=previous, its bucket edges in the viewer's zone, and its read budget.

Risks

  • It reads twice the days. A warm platform read costs about one stored day per workspace per day, plus the 24 live counts (docs/outcomes.md, "The read budget"). With three workspaces, 26 weeks against 26 is 364 days, about 1,120 reads, against about 570 for the period alone. A month view is two years of days, about 2,220. A cold first Run past the 5,000-read budget leaves buckets unread (F5), and the count grows with every workspace.
  • The route needs work. The previous section of GET /v1/outcomes carries outcome totals only (succeeded, failed, rate). This proposal needs the usage block per bucket and per group for the previous span too: an apps/swarm-api change, no frozen-contract change.
  • A young platform has little "before". On dev, the period before a 12-month view is mostly days with no agents. Those are measured zeros, so the ghost lies on the zero rule and every group reads "new", which is true but says little.
  • Two lines per chart double the ink; at 300 wide they cross often. Holidays break the week-1-against-week-1 alignment.
  • With a bucket unread, E compares over the buckets read in both periods (F5). The Timeline instead drops the change. The two rules would differ until one is chosen for both.
