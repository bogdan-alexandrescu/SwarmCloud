Platform counts over time: A, the Analyst dashboard

BGD asked on 2026-10-23 that Platform counts “also show the data in a nice set of visualizations like graphs and charts” (#2149). Five lanes draw five dashboards; this is A. It reads like an analyst’s report: five numbers you can quote, each with its trend and its change, then one large chart of how many agents ran and one of what they cost, then a table that holds every figure as text.

All data is example data: the user is “Operator”, the workspaces are eng, smoke and u-operator, the pool accounts acct-claude-01…acct-claude-05. Every frame is computed from one example year of daily figures, so a week, a month and a day agree. The frames follow the Light / Dark / System toggle at the top of the page and in each frame’s user footer. Desktop frames are drawn at the window’s width (1424 in a 1440 window) and scroll inside their own box below 1180; on a phone they give way to a one-line note, because the phone frames are the 390 view.

AAnalyst dashboard

Idea

A KPI row on top: agents, retries, run hours, tokens and estimated cost, each with a sparkline and its change against the previous full bucket. Under it, a large stacked area of agents over time, coloured by the chosen breakdown, with retries on their own lane. Under that, the estimated cost per bucket with its coverage lane, beside cost per agent. A breakdown table closes the section. One priced Run reads all of it, and the live state counts the page already has.

Pros

  • Answers the acceptance question (“by week, by agent type”: agents with retries apart, run hours, tokens, cost with coverage) on one screen, F3.
  • The five tiles are the figures someone quotes in a message, and each carries its own coverage line, so a quoted floor stays a floor.
  • Volume and money are separate charts on separate scales: a cheap, busy week never looks like an expensive one.
  • The table is the copyable, accessible version of every mark; the charts add the shape only.
  • One Run, one price, and a breakdown costs no read.

Risks

  • A stacked area reads exactly only at its bottom layer; a small layer on top (mock, browser) is hard to compare week to week. The readout and the table carry the exact numbers.
  • Five series is the palette’s limit (§1.6). Pool account has seven keys, so two accounts share a colour in the chart and are apart in the table.
  • The Agents and Cost sparklines restate the big charts in miniature. They are glances, not a second reading; dropping those two is the cheapest cut if it reads as repetition.
  • “Change” is the last full bucket against the one before, not the span against the previous span: that comparison would double the read. The tile says which it is.
  • Five tiles make a long first screen at 390 before the first chart (F7).

F3’s state, by agent type, at 390. Nothing scrolls sideways: every chart is drawn again for its width (340), not scaled, so tick text stays at 12px; the x labels thin out by stride, anchored on the newest bucket; the legend moves under the chart; the cost charts stack; the tiles go to two columns with the cost tile full width; the table becomes one card per row.

Reflow rules

  • Drawings: 1032 for a full-width chart, 708 beside a legend, 640 and 368 for the cost pair, 340 on a phone. The sheet shows the drawing whose authored width the box holds.
  • Labels: one every n buckets so labels stay at least 52px apart on a phone and 64px on desktop, always including the newest bucket.
  • Lane labels shorten (“agents per week” to “agents”, “cost reported by: all ended agents (solid) or not all (open)” to “cost reported by”, with the legend saying the rest) rather than shrink.
  • Tiles: two columns; the cost tile spans both, because its coverage line is the longest.
  • Readout: pinned under the chart on tap (F6), never a floating tooltip that covers the marks under a finger.
  • Table: one card per row, with the same dashes and “at least” as the desktop table.
  • Ticks and state counts: unchanged, stacked (F1 and F2 phones).

·What is not decided here

This is a proposal: nothing is built until BGD picks one of the five. Two choices inside A are open even if A is picked: whether the Agents and Cost tiles keep their sparklines (Risks, third point), and whether “change” should compare the span against the previous span at twice the read.
