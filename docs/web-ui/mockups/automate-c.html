Automate, proposal C: Time first

One of four competing proposals (A to D) for BGD's redesign of the console's Automate section. BGD picks frames across the four on the picks page. Every frame is drawn at 1440 wide with the console's nav open, or at 390 for the phone. The theme control at the top repaints the page and every frame, light or dark.

Idea

Automate is read against time. The landing is a swimlane with one lane per schedule on one shared axis: the last 7 days on the left and the next 24 hours, magnified, on the right of a "now" line. Each past firing is a dot coloured by its outcome. Each next firing is a hollow ring. Under every lane, a band shows each day's spend against the per-day cap. An approval that blocks a firing is pinned where it blocks: a dashed band runs from its request toward its expiry, and the firings it holds up are struck through. Below the lanes comes a master-detail layout. The lanes fold into a list on the left, and the chosen schedule or approval opens on the right with its own lane drawn tall. One approval shows where it sits in its schedule's timeline, and what each answer does to the next firing.

How it differs from the console today

  • Today, Schedules is a table: a row with a next run and a last outcome. Here, the lane shows a week of outcomes at once, so "failing for two nights" or "paused since Thursday" can be seen at a glance, without opening the schedule.
  • Today, approvals sit in a separate inbox, and "Waiting on you" is a card. Here, each waiting approval is also drawn on its schedule's lane, at the firing it holds up.
  • Today, a schedule's page has five tabs (history, budget, gate, settings, audit). Here it is one page, beside the list, opened by its lane: history and budget are drawn on the lane, and gate and settings are two cards. Audit is a link.
  • Automate keeps its two pages, Schedules and Approvals (SD1, docs/schedules.md §6.1). Nothing moves in the nav.

Risks

  • A schedule that fires every 15 or 30 minutes has too many dots for 7 days. The lane would draw it as a strip with an outcome mix. That is not drawn here: the busiest lane fires every 4 hours.
  • The axis changes scale at "now" (a week on the left, a day on the right). The zone labels say so, but it can still be misread.
  • "What the next firing does" while an approval waits is a reading the API does not serve yet. It is listed at the end as a new field and must come from the server, not from the UI guessing the overlap rule.
  • On a phone the lanes stack. Each lane's words go above its track, and the pinned notes become words.
What every frame obeys
  • PICKS.md: nothing below 12px, no all-caps, no letter-spacing, and a light / dark / system toggle (in the page bar, and in every frame's panel foot). An unknown figure is a dash with its reason, never 0. State words are lower-case words ("waiting", "approved"), never raw enums.
  • The V2 Sky spine and the house style of Config E (#2157) and Subscription accounts A (#2222): tokens, type scale, lead strip, tables, cards, chips and buttons. Only the lanes are new, and they are drawn the way account health's proposal B draws its lanes.
  • The time is Mon 26 Oct 2026, 09:00Z. The approval apr_31f74dc3dc84349da9b83697 and its schedule, run, issue and pull request are the real ones. The other schedules and approvals are example data on the same repository.

+New fields this proposal needs

Everything else a frame shows is served today by GET /v1/schedules, /v1/schedules/{id}, /firings, /spans, /audit, POST /v1/schedules:preview, GET /v1/schedule-types and GET /v1/approvals[/{id}]. None of these needs a change to the frozen contract (apps/common/swarm_common/): they are swarm-api read shapes.

Words. The API's approval state rejected is drawn as "denied", because the button BGD asked for is Deny. A schedule's enabled state is drawn as "active", and auto_paused is drawn as "paused by its tripwire" when its pause.code is RUN_OVER_BUDGET, or "paused after failures" for CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES. A held merge (hold.code: NEEDS_OWNER) is "held for the platform owner", as Approvals.tsx says it today.

Assumed, not read. Failure review’s policy.overlap is drawn as skip, which is why its firings are skipped while #2392 waits. The live schedule may say otherwise. Under the blocks field above, the server would answer this instead of the drawing.
