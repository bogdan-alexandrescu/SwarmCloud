The log dock and the content viewers

Inside the agent split view: a live log you can follow, pause, search and wrap, that jumps to the next error, says how much it masked and says where output is missing; an attempt switcher by generation; a viewer per kind of file; checkpoints with their contents. The API already serves almost all of it. The console draws about half.

What the gap notice is computed from

Each live read returns tail_window.object_offset (where this window starts in the whole stream) and the window's length. The console keeps the previous read's end per stream. If the next read starts after it, the bytes in between were published and overwritten before anyone read them: output missing between byte A and byte B, with both reads' published_at. Without the header (a tail from before #184) the console says it cannot tell, not that nothing is missing.

What I could not find or ground

  • No error classifier exists server-side, so "jump to error" is a console-side pattern match (Traceback, ERROR, FAILED, exit codes) plus transcript is_error. It can miss and over-match; the button says what it matches.
  • Search covers the window loaded (256 KiB live, or the final record's pages read so far), not the whole stream. The count says "this window".
  • A log dock would sit above the existing telemetry dock (Dock.tsx). Two bottom docks is a naming conflict to settle; the frames keep the telemetry line unchanged.
  • No measured figure for how often gaps happen at a 5 s poll and a 256 KiB tail.

ABottom dock under the detail

The log docks along the bottom of the detail column and stays open as you move between Details, Attempts, Artifacts and Checkpoints. The list keeps its full height. Drag the top edge to resize, or shrink it to a one-line strip. Agents, logs and sizes are example data.

Variant A

Bottom dock

Recommended

Idea

A dock is the one place in a split view that can stay put while the rest changes. Logs are what people watch while they read something else, so the log gets the dock and the tabs stay as they are.

Pros

  • You can follow a running agent while reading its checkpoints or artifacts
  • Keeps the four tabs and their routes exactly as decided
  • Same gesture as the telemetry dock: drag to resize, one-line strip when small
  • On a phone it becomes a bottom sheet, the common pattern there

Risks

  • Steals height from the detail on short screens; needs the one-line strip and a remembered height
  • Two docks at the bottom of the frame (logs and API reads) must look clearly different
  • Follow and pause need real scroll anchoring, or the dock fights the reader

BA third column for the log

Variant B

Third column

Idea

Logs are long and narrow, so give them a full-height column of their own beside the detail.

Pros

  • Full height for the log: many more lines visible at once
  • Detail and log side by side, nothing overlaps

Risks

  • Needs about 1440px; below that the list has to collapse, which breaks the 380px / 50% divider stops
  • Log lines are wide (paths, tracebacks); 440px forces wrap or sideways scroll
  • Three columns on the phone have no natural form; it ends up being variant C there

CLogs as a fifth tab in the detail

Variant C

Tab inside the detail

Idea

Logs become a fifth tab beside Details, Attempts, Artifacts and Checkpoints, with its own route. Closest to today, where logs sit in Artifacts › Logs.

Pros

  • Smallest change: TaskPane gains one member, the log gets a route
  • All the height and width of the detail
  • Simplest phone form: the tab is the page

Risks

  • You cannot watch the log and read anything else; switching tabs loses your place
  • Five tabs crowd the strip on a phone
  • Follow keeps polling while the tab is hidden, or it has to stop and catch up

VThe viewers, one per kind

The same viewers in every variant, in the Artifacts tab, in a checkpoint's contents, and on the phone. The kind is the server's (kind from the name table, then the NUL check); the viewer never decides by guessing. Every viewer footer says the size, whether this is the whole file, and how many values were masked.

SEvery state, in its honest form

Loading, empty, missing, failed, not applicable, not served and stale are seven different things, and each gets its own form. None of them is drawn as an empty log or a zero.

RRecommendation

Variant A, the bottom dock

Watching a running agent and reading its work are done at the same time. A dock lets the log stay put while the tabs change, keeps the four tabs and routes already decided, and becomes a bottom sheet on the phone. B needs 1320px and fights the divider's stops; C is the smallest change but makes you choose between the log and everything else. Most of the work is in the console: the API already serves the tail header, the masked count, attempts by id and all three checkpoint reads.

Small choices made in the frames

  • The dock spans the detail column only; the list keeps its full height, because the log belongs to the selected agent.
  • The dock opens on Transcript for claude-code and codex, and on Runner for runners with no agent CLI (browser, generic, mock).
  • Follow is on for a running attempt and off for a final record. Scrolling up pauses; a pill counts the lines that arrived while paused.
  • Keys: F follow, W wrap, E next error, / search; [ stays the list toggle.
  • The attempt switcher is labelled by generation (gen 3, gen 2, gen 1) with the attempt id and its end state beside it, because generation is what fencing reasons about.
  • The gap notice is amber and dashed, inline at the point of the gap, giving the bytes and the two publish times. It is never merged into the lines around it.
  • Masked values show as ******** in amber; the count sits in the dock's facts row and in each viewer footer, plain at zero.
  • Dock height is remembered per viewer (as panes.ts does), clamped 120px to 70%, with a one-line strip below that.
  • Large files: the first 512 KiB window, a "next window from byte N" button, and download. Nothing is stitched without saying so.
  • Images say not redacted, binaries download only, archives say not redacted: the API's own X-Swarm-Redaction words.

Open, for the owner

  • What to call the two docks: the log dock and the API-reads telemetry line both sit at the bottom.
  • Whether "jump to error" can rely on a console-side pattern or should wait for the API to mark error lines.
  • Whether swarm-api's 300 s request timeout, which caps archive downloads, should be raised (already an open item in redesign-v2 §6 S3).
