The repository graph's overlays: four ways to show communities, an issue's territory and the work already in it

Row KG8 of knowledge-graph.md §6 builds the console's community layer and its issue and lane overlays, and asks for the owner's pick from a mock-up first, as RI13 did. These are four complete answers. Each is drawn in the Structure view and the Network view that GR3 built (PR #929), on desktop and on a 390px phone, in light and dark. The costs, the recommendation and the owner's questions are in docs/design/graph-overlays.md.

What is real and what is example data. The graph is this repository's own: 339 modules and 1,575 import edges in apps/ at 2887032, extracted from the Python and TypeScript imports. The 25 communities come from Louvain clustering of that graph (resolution 3, seed 7), standing in for KG2's clustering, which is not built. Each is named after its most-imported module. The territory is computed the way knowledge-graph.md §4.1 describes it. The declared files are the three a plan would name for the example issue. The expanded territory is their importers and imports at depth 1. The seams are the expanded modules with fan-in of 47 or more. The issue, the two lanes' states and the second PR are example data. #929 is the real GR3 PR, and its file list is real. The Network frames are drawn as SVG with a precomputed force layout, to look like GR3's vis-network canvas. They are not vis-network itself.

Every variant uses the same marks, so the variants differ in how they arrange the graph, not in what a mark means. A solid ink ring marks a declared file, a dashed ring the expanded territory and a diamond a seam. A red ring marks a collision and a dashed amber ring an overlap. A letter badge (A–D) names the lane. None of these spends the accent: selection is an ink ring, as design-system.md §1.3 requires. Colour is never the only channel, because every frame lists the same overlap in words beside the graph.

1Paint: every layer is a channel on the flat graph

Colour by community; rings for the territory; badges for lanes

Variant 1

Today's graph, untouched in shape, with three paint layers. Each node is filled with its community's hue. In Structure, the clusters become community boxes outlined in the same hue. The territory and the lanes are rings and badges on the nodes where they fall. Nothing moves when a layer is switched on.

Optimises

Seeing everything at once, with nothing hidden and nothing to learn. It is the cheapest variant to build.

Costs

  • About +3 kB gz (estimate)
  • No new route beyond KG3's
  • Needs a hue per community

Risks

  • Breaks design-system.md §1.6. There are five series identities, and this graph has 25 communities, so the eight hues drawn here repeat. Two communities share a colour and cannot be told apart in greyscale.
  • At 2,000 modules the territory is about 37 rings in a hairball (graph-rendering.md §3.4).

2Regions: a community is a region you open, and the issue opens the ones it lands in

Aggregated by community; the territory decides what is open

recommendedVariant 2

The graph starts aggregated: one region per community, as knowledge-graph.md §4.7 asks (collapse a community to a node) and as graph-rendering.md §3.4 requires past about 500 nodes. A closed region shows its size, how much of the territory it holds and which lanes touch it. The communities that hold a declared file open by themselves and show their modules with the territory marks. In Network, a closed community is one circle on the community meta-graph, with edge width counting the imports between communities. An open one spreads its modules inside a hull. A side list gives the territory by community and opens or closes each one.

Optimises

Scale and the plan-approval question: "which parts of the codebase does this issue touch, and who is already there?" The answer is the open regions plus their badges.

Costs

  • About +5 kB gz (estimate): GR3's aggregation, keyed by community instead of directory
  • The meta-graph needs KG3's /communities to carry cross-community edge counts
  • Network hands vis-network the meta-graph already aggregated: 126 ms to build at 2,034 modules. vis-network's own cluster(), called once per community, took 41 s (§5)

Risks

  • A territory file in a closed region is a count, not a node, until opened.
  • The regions move if KG2's communities drift between index runs. KG2's acceptance (Jaccard ≥ 0.9) bounds this.

3Lens: the territory is the drawing, and everything else steps back

A territory map in Structure; a dimming lens in Network

Variant 3

When an issue is chosen, Structure stops drawing the repository and draws only the territory, read left to right. The declared files and seams come first, then the expanded territory grouped by community, then the lanes and PRs, with a line from each lane to the files it shares. Red lines are collisions and amber lines are overlaps. Network keeps the whole graph but dims everything outside the territory, and draws a faint hull for each community the territory crosses.

Optimises

Reading one issue's territory and its collisions as a list with structure, which is the plan-review moment. Its size depends on the territory, not on the repository.

Costs

  • About +6 kB gz (estimate): a second Structure layout, the layered territory map
  • Network costs a style pass only

Risks

  • Structure no longer shows the repository while an issue is chosen, so the community layer is only grouping there.
  • Two Structure drawings to keep in step.

4Matrix: communities × work, with the graph as the map beside it

A table that answers first; the graph shows where a cell is

Variant 4

The Graph tab leads with a matrix. Each row is a community the territory or a lane touches. The columns are the issue's declared, expanded and seam counts, then one column per lane or PR. A red cell means the lane holds a declared file in that community, and an amber cell means it holds another territory file. Clicking a cell rings its modules on the small graph beside the table, in Structure or Network, and lists them underneath.

Optimises

A precise, accessible and testable answer. It is the best phone story, and it suits screen readers and DOM tests, because the answer is a table.

Costs

  • About +4 kB gz (estimate)
  • The table renders in milliseconds at any size
  • Reuses the shipped Table component

Risks

  • The graph becomes secondary, and the owner asked for a graph layer.
  • A matrix shows counts, not shape, so a seam's reach is not visible.

5Side by side, with costs

The render times are measured at 2,034 modules and 9,733 edges. That graph is this repository's graph copied six times, with 3% random edges added between the copies, giving 150 communities. The mock-up's own renderers drew it (the functions on this page), in headless Chromium 141 with software rendering on a 6-vCPU container. Each figure is the median of 5 runs, from building the markup to two animation frames later. The Network figures are vis-network 10.0.2 on the same graph, with positions given and physics off: the cost of drawing and updating, not of settling. graph-rendering.md §3.4 measured settling at 7,246 ms for 2,000 modules. The bundle row has a measured part and an estimate. The measured part is the gzipped size of this page's minified renderer for the variant, plus 2.1 kB of marks and overlap list shared by all four. The estimate is what the built TSX will cost, with types, keyboard and tests: about twice that.

6Recommendation

Variant 2, Regions, with variant 4's matrix as the phone view

Regions is the only variant that answers all three layers at 2,000 modules without a hairball. It is also the shape both design docs already chose: knowledge-graph.md §4.7 asks to collapse a community to a node, and graph-rendering.md §3.4 says the explorer must start aggregated and open by focus. Opening the communities that hold a declared file is that focus. It needs one hue at most, so it stays inside the five series of design-system.md §1.6. On a phone, its territory-by-community cards carry variant 4's per-lane counts.

The five owner questions (variant, phone, what opens by default, overlap severity, where the overlays are reached from) are in graph-overlays.md §5 and in the run's questions.json.
