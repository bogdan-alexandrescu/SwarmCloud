AAnswer first

Idea

The first thing on the page is the question an operator came with: can the pool take another agent, and if not, when? One big number (“0 of 7”), one sentence naming the account that frees first and when, and on the right how many agents are waiting, linked to them. Under it, one table ranked by when each account can take an agent, grouped as “Can take now”, “Frees when an agent finishes” and “Waits for a window”. Each row says, in words, what blocks it and when that clears, with both windows drawn against their floors. A forecast card says when the pool goes dark at this pace and when it comes back. Everything else about one account (holds, loans, organisation, credential, actions) is in a side sheet.

Pros

  • It answers “why are my agents waiting?” in the first line, and nothing else on the page can contradict it: the number is the count of rows in the first group.
  • The rank order is the order the broker’s own next_eligible_at gives, so the top row is the next account the pool will use.
  • Each row reads its block in words (“7-day window: 14% left, under its 15% floor”), with the floor drawn as a tick on the bar, so the 15% / 5% rule can be seen, not just known.
  • A reset window is drawn as reset, a dashed empty bar, and never shows its old figure.
  • The table keeps the built split view’s address (?account=): the side sheet is that pane, opened over the table rather than beside it.

Risks

  • Ranking moves rows whenever a window resets, so an account is not always in the same place. Searching by name, or the address, finds it; the built page sorts by id.
  • A side sheet covers the right of the table, so you cannot read one account’s detail beside another’s row. The built page’s split view could.
  • The forecast needs a pace, and the broker serves none yet. F2 draws one as an example; built today, the card would have only the reset times (which are facts), and its “goes dark” line would be a dash with its reason.
  • Nine columns at 1072 px is tight. The organisation lives under the name, not in a column of its own.
  • One large number invites being quoted. It is right only for the tenant reading it: an account not lent to you is not in your 7.

·Decided and open

Decided in this proposal

  • The page opens on the verdict, “N of M accounts can take a new agent”, and the account that frees first. “Available” as a count is gone: the state (paused, draining, reauth) is a reason in the blocks column, not a headline.
  • Rank order: can take now (most free slots, then most headroom), then full accounts (no clock), then by next_eligible_at. Paused, draining and reauth accounts would go last, in a group of their own.
  • A reset window reads “reset HH:MMZ · nothing read since”, with a dashed empty bar. “Nothing read since” rather than BGD’s “nothing used since”: the broker counts the window as unused, but whether it really was is not known until the next reading.
  • The floor is a tick on every bar, and named in words in the blocks column. The rule line under the answer names all four parts of the rule (two floors, the halving, the cap).
  • A window the console does not know by name is a tag under the account, held to the default floor, never hidden.
  • Detail is a side sheet over the table on desktop and a full screen on a phone, at the address the built page already uses.

Open, for BGD

  • The organisation and plan, and the duplicate flag, need POOL-DUP-DETECT to serve them. Until it does, should the page show a dash, as drawn here, or hide the line?
  • The forecast needs a pace the broker does not serve. Is the forecast card worth a broker change (burn per hour per window, from the readings it already stores), or should A ship with resets only?
  • Should the waiting count include the later workflow steps that wait on the parked agents (32 on 2026-10-24), or only the 28 that wait on the pool itself? Drawn: 28, with the 32 as a second line.
  • Whether a full account outranks one whose window clears in ten minutes. Drawn: it does, because it can free at any moment.

This is a proposal: nothing is built until BGD picks one of the four.
