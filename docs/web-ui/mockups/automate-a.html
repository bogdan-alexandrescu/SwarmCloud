Automate, proposal A: List and side sheet

One of four competing proposals (A to D) for BGD's redesign of the console's Automate section. Each draws every Automate screen under the same frame ids (A1 to A7), so the picks page can mix frames across them. This one pushes Subscription accounts A's pattern further: the list is the page, and one schedule or one approval opens in a side sheet over it.

Idea

Automate keeps its two tabs, Schedules and Approvals. Each opens on a one-sentence lead that names what needs the reader, with one button, and then one dense house table. A schedule (A2) or an approval (A5) opens in a right side sheet over its list, as an account does on Subscription accounts, so the list stays in view and the next row is one click away. Creating or editing a schedule (A3) is the same sheet, stepped: type, target, cadence, gates, budget, then review, with each step's refusal shown where the server would raise it.

How it differs from the console today

  • Today a schedule is a page of its own with five tabs (History, Budget, Gate, Settings, Audit). Here it is one sheet read top to bottom, with the list still beside it.
  • Today Approvals is a list and detail split. Here the list is full width, oldest first with time to expiry, and an item opens in the sheet.
  • Today the State column shows the API's four states. Here it shows six words that tell the reader what to do: active, paused, paused by its tripwire, waiting on approval, failing, never fired.
  • Today the editor is one long form. Here it is six steps, and the review step shows the next five firings and what the gate will hold.
  • Today Approve and Reject are offered to every member (a viewer sees them disabled) and the server refuses the rest. Here the sheet says who can decide, and offers Approve only to them (a new field, listed at the end).

Pros and risks

  • Pro: one place to look per tab. The lead says what needs you, and the list never goes away.
  • Pro: the sheet keeps the built addresses (/automate/schedules/<id>, /approvals/<id>), opened over the list instead of in place of it.
  • Risk: the sheet covers the right of the list, so you cannot compare two schedules' histories side by side.
  • Risk: a stepped editor is slower for someone changing one field. Edit opens on the step that holds the field, and Review is always one click away.
What every frame obeys
  • PICKS.md: nothing below 12px, no all-caps, no letter-spacing, a light / dark / system toggle (top of this page and in every frame's panel foot), and an unknown figure is a dash with its reason, never 0. State words are lower-case words, never the API's enums.
  • The V2 Sky spine, Config E's tokens, lead strip, cards, chips and buttons, and Subscription accounts A's side sheet. Nothing here is a new visual language.
  • Example data, read at Monday 2026-10-26 09:00Z, except A5's approval, whose fields are the real apr_31f74dc3dc84349da9b83697's. The viewer is Operator, the platform owner, unless a frame says otherwise.

A1

Automate's landing: Schedules

Automate opens on Schedules. The lead names what needs the reader across both tabs; the table has every state, with the ones that need a look tinted; under it, what waits and what was decided this week.

A2

One schedule, in the side sheet over the list

Nightly issue sweep, opened from A1's list. What it does, its type and target, cadence and next firings, its firings with each one's runs and pull requests, spend against the day budget and the per-run tripwire, the gate and who approves, and its actions.

A3

A new or edited schedule: the stepped sheet

The same sheet, in six steps: type, target, cadence, gates, budget, review. Each step checks what the server checks at that step, so a refusal shows on the step that caused it, not as one error after Create. Steps already passed are ticked; a step with a refusal is marked.

Every validation message drawn is one the built editor or the API already gives: interval_too_short with min_interval_minutes, budget_above_cap, invalid_budget (a day under one run), "Choose at least one repository.", and schedule_changed on a stale revision. The preview is POST /v1/schedules:preview.

A4

Approvals

The second tab. Waiting first, oldest first, each with its time to expiry and who may decide; then decided (approved or rejected, by whom, when, with the reason); then expired and superseded. The kind filter is the API's ?kind=.

A5

One approval: apr_31f74dc3dc84349da9b83697, in six states

The merge of PR #2392, asked for by the failure-review schedule's firing. Pending is drawn over the Approvals list; the other five states are the same sheet alone. Approve sends the digest it shows ({head_sha: dbd00f06c5cd, verdict: MERGE}), so it can only ever approve the head the reader saw.

A6

Empty and honest states

What the tabs say when there is nothing, when the deployment does not serve Automate, and when a read fails. A count that was not read is a dash with its reason; a 0 is drawn only when the list was read and is empty.

A7

Phone at 390: A1, A2 and A5

On a phone the table's rows become cards, and the side sheet becomes the whole screen with Back to the list. The order of the sheet does not change.

New fields this proposal needs

Every other figure on this page is served today by /v1/schedules, /v1/schedule-types, /v1/schedules:preview or /v1/approvals, or is derived from what they serve (the six state words, time to expiry, the counts). These are not, and the frames that draw them need them:
