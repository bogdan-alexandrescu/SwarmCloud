Wide workflows: many stages, many agents per stage

The owner asked how to draw a workflow with several stages and a handful of agents or more in each. This page answers with mock-ups built on what is live: the approved Workflows V2 page and its Graph. Three ways to draw it, then the list row, the selected step, the collapsed rail, a phone, and the honest states. Workflows, steps, ids, costs and times are example data; the signed-in user is "Operator".

Two corrections to the question as asked.

1. Today's graph does not have stage columns. It has stage rows: the graph descends, and the steps of a stage sit side by side. That is what the owner's note at Workflows.tsx:184 asked for, and the note says "displayed horizontally not vertically", not "in a vertical stack". So Variant A below is today's rows, and Variant B is rows that wrap into a grid, which is the one thing a recorded decision (2026-09-30) says a level must not do.

2. A stage opened by the reader is kept across a 10 s poll today, not 5 s: the comment quoting "5 seconds" predates WORKFLOW_POLL_MS = 10_000.

2The three example workflows

All three are shapes the API accepts today. Each is drawn at one moment, labelled example data.

(1) wf_ex-review-wave · 1 → 8 → 8 → 8 → 1

plan, 8 implementers, 8 reviews (one per implementer), 8 fixes gated on each review's verdict, integrate. Under on_step_failure: continue.

At this moment: impl-6 failed, so review-6, fix-6 and integrate were cancelled before they started. fix-1 runs (NOT_YET); fix-2's agent was skipped (MERGE).

(2) wf_ex-shard-audit · 1 → 24 → 1

prep writes shards.json; 24 codex shards; reduce stages every shard's findings.json with input_layout: by_parent.

At this moment: 13 running and 2 starting hold 15 slots; 3 queued, 4 parked on provider quota and 2 ready on a full class hold nothing.

(3) wf_ex-billing-split · 2 → 6 → 4 → 1

Two roots. e2e-auth waits for api-auth and ui-shell only; docs for api-search only; review-migrate for migrate only.

At this moment: e2e-auth already runs while api-billing, api-search and ui-forms, on the level above, still run. A level is depth, not a gate.

3Variant A: today's stage rows, wide stages as bands that open in place

Variant A

Stage rows with summarised bands (extends what is live)

Recommended

Idea

Keep the live Graph: stages descend as rows, and a stage too wide for the column folds into a band that opens in place to its real width, scrolling sideways. Add four things to the band and the edges: a mix bar (state proportions at a glance); the failed, then running, then parked steps named as chips, each one a pick target; slots held versus waiting (invariant 1: only LEASED to RUNNING hold capacity); and edges that bundle at rest and light up on hover or select. When two opened stages are 1:1 (review-k of impl-k), children are ordered under their parent so every edge is a straight lane.

Pros

  • Extends the code that ships: StageBand, stageCensus, edgeKinds and the opened-stage store stay; the additions are rendering.
  • Keeps the owner's 2026-09-30 rule that a level never wraps, and the 2026-09-2x top-to-bottom flow.
  • A failure is visible without opening anything, by name, not only by count.
  • Works for 49 (the API's ceiling for one stage) the same way as for 8.

Risks

  • An opened 24-wide stage is still a long sideways scroll; the minimap is the only overview while it is open.
  • Named chips clip: at five names plus "+N more", the band leans on the counts and the label for the rest.
  • Hover highlighting needs a keyboard equivalent (focus does the same), or it is mouse-only.
  • Ordering 1:1 children under parents changes the order a reader sees inside a stage from step order to parent order.

4Variant B: stages as rows of compact tiles, edges only on selection

The question attributes this to the owner's note near Workflows.tsx:184. The note asks for the steps of a stage side by side ("horizontally not vertically"), which is what A already does. B differs from A in two ways: tiles instead of cards, and a stage that wraps onto more lines instead of scrolling.

Variant B

Stage rows that wrap into a grid of tiles

Idea

Every stage is a framed box: a header with its name, count, mix bar and counts, then a wrap-grid of 150×30 tiles (mark, name, one short fact). Nothing folds and nothing scrolls sideways: at 1440 a stage holds six tiles a line, so 24 steps are four lines and 8 are a line of six and a line of two. Edges are not drawn at rest; selecting a step draws its lineage and dims the rest.

Pros

  • Everything is on screen at once; no band to open, no sideways scroll, no minimap.
  • Dense: (1) at 26 steps and (2) at 26 steps both fit one 1440 screen without zooming.
  • The frame around a stage is what makes a wrapped stage read as one stage.

Risks

  • Goes against a recorded owner decision (2026-09-30, dag.ts:1629): "a wrapped level reads as two dependency levels". The frame mitigates it; it does not remove it.
  • With no edges at rest, (3)'s partial dependencies are invisible until you select something: the graph stops saying which steps wait on which.
  • A tile has room for one fact. Runner, time and the why-line move to hover or the side panel.
  • Replaces the canvas, its layout arithmetic (layoutOf, autoTier) and the zoom tiers. The most new code of the three.

5Variant C: a stage × state matrix with drill-down

Variant C

Stage × state matrix, drilling into a filtered step table

Idea

A fourth view beside Graph, Table and Timeline: one row per stage, one column per state, a count in each cell, and the slots each stage holds. Clicking a count filters the existing step table to that stage and state. No edges, no layout.

Pros

  • Scales to the API ceiling (50 steps) and any width without layout work: it is a table.
  • Answers "where is it stuck and how many" faster than any drawing; the drill-down lands on rows with their why-line.
  • Reuses the Table view and its rows, so a step reads the same everywhere.

Risks

  • Throws away the shape: (3)'s partial dependencies cannot be seen at all, and "stage" for (3) is only depth.
  • Eleven state columns are wide; most are 0 for most workflows.
  • A fourth view is one more thing to choose; the Graph is still what people open first.

6The list row's stage-shape glyph

Today the Shape column is text, 1 → 5 → 1, and the code records why there is no picture: a 60px thumbnail of a graph "resolves into a smudge" (Workflows.tsx:1348-1354). This glyph is not a graph. It is one column per stage, taller for a wider stage (log scale up to the API's 50), filled with that stage's state mix. The text stays beside it. A "partial" chip marks a workflow where some step depends on part of the level above, which the text cannot say.

7Opening a step from the workflow

Picking a node or a chip on a band fills the side panel in place (live since WF-7); nothing on the canvas navigates. The panel names what the step waits for and feeds, and carries Open agent →, which goes to the Agents split view.

8With the panel collapsed

Collapsing keeps the 84px spine and gives the canvas 236px more. The bands and the open stage behave the same; more of an opened stage is on screen before it scrolls.

9Phone, 390px, for Variant A

On a phone a stage becomes a card (the approved stacked stage cards), and here, only here, an opened stage is a vertical list: on a narrow screen a list is what a reader already knows how to read. Picking a step opens a sheet.

Each state in its own form, in both themes. A missing figure is a dash with a reason, never 0.

11Recommendation

Variant A, and it extends what is live rather than replacing it

Keep today's Graph: stages as rows, top to bottom, wide stages folded into bands that open in place, opened stages kept across the 10 s poll. It already follows both owner decisions on this canvas (top-to-bottom flow; a level never wraps) and already bundles edges at a band. What is missing is on the band and the edges, not in the layout.

Take one piece of C: clicking a count on a band ("3 running") opens the existing Table filtered to that stage and state. That is C's drill-down without a fourth view. B is the only option that needs a decision reversed (the no-wrap rule), so it is not recommended; if that rule is reopened, B becomes tenable.

Small choices made in the frames

  • The mix bar orders segments by what needs eyes: failed, holding capacity, parked, waiting, not read, succeeded, skipped, cancelled. The counts keep the live order (failures, cancels, not read, then by size).
  • A band names at most five steps, failed first, then running, then parked, then "+N more". Succeeded steps are never named on a band.
  • "holds N slots · M waiting hold none" sits on every band whose stage holds anything.
  • Edges into the same step share one lane between two levels (lanes are offset per step, so two steps' edges never merge); hover or keyboard focus lights the step's own edges and fades the rest. The selection uses the accent, never a state colour.
  • When two opened stages are 1:1, children are ordered under their parents so each edge is a vertical lane.
  • A verdict-skipped step gets a dashed check and a striped card, read from result_summary.verdict_gate.agent_ran; it stays SUCCEEDED in every count.
  • The list keeps its text shape and gains the stage glyph plus a "partial" chip.
  • On a phone, an opened stage is a vertical list; on a desktop it never is.
  • Rail labels keep the live words ("starts", "then ×8"); the band title shows the first and last step ids, not an invented stage name.
What could not be grounded in the code
  • The Names tier's node width is measured from step names (nodeWidthAt), so how many Names nodes fit is not stated here; the frames draw Details-size nodes throughout.
  • Whether a step id prefix ("impl-*") could name a stage is not something the code does; the frames do not claim it.
  • The 1:1 ordering, mix bar, named chips, hover highlight, verdict-skip look, matrix and glyph are proposals; none exists on main.
  • Integrate in (1) cannot stage the gated fixes' output today (docs/workflows.md:518); the example is runnable as drawn, but that data flow is not available.
