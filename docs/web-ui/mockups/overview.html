Layout O1

Lead and ledger

Owner's pick · 2026-10-01

Idea

Keep today's order (the checks first, then the state of the platform) and add the two things it lacks: a three-part lifecycle band (waiting, holding capacity, finished today) with every state counted under its own mark, and a "Waiting, and why" card that groups by reason. Below that, Running beside Cost, Waiting beside Headroom, then Recent failures.

Pros

  • Every one of the five questions has one card, in reading order.
  • PARKED, READY and QUEUED are told apart by mark, by word and by reason.
  • It is the smallest change from today's screen, so the existing tests and the prose budget carry over.

Risks

  • It is the longest of the three, at about 1.4 screens at 1440×900.
  • The checks and Headroom both mention class:browser. The check has to link to the pool rather than restate the numbers, or the page says the same thing twice.

BDecisions

Menu: Sky spineThe 84px spine with a 44px bright Hive, Submit and the four sections; the 236px panel lists Overview's regions as jump links. See navigation.html.
Mark: HiveFull cut on the spine at 32px, compact cut in the phone header and the favicon. See brand.html.
Inspector: split view"Running now", "Waiting, and why" and "Recent failures" rows open /agents/<tab>/<id> with the list on the left and the agent on the right.
Headroom links to the matrix"Pools →" on the Headroom card opens Pools › By runner profile, the profile-by-pool matrix with the binding cell outlined. See capacity.html.

Why this layout

  • It answers the five questions in the order people ask them: what needs a look, how much is waiting versus working versus done, what is running and costing, why the rest waits, and whether there is room.
  • The lifecycle band makes invariant 1 visible on the landing page: 37 waiting cost nothing, 12 hold capacity.
  • "Waiting, and why" groups by reason, not by agent, so 22 queued agents behind two workflows read as one line. Each line opens /agents/waiting filtered to that reason.
  • Reads stay at eight. The band reuses /v1/stats (exact counts per state, 60s); waiting reasons and failures come from the task page already read every 20s. No new endpoint is needed.

CWhat exists today

Today's Overview, from Overview.tsx

  • Eight reads, each owning its own card state: capacity, the task page, leases, providers, accounts and workflows every 20s, /v1/stats every 60s, and spend.
  • Head: "Overview", then a facts strip: scope (tenant), a reads tally ("8/8" with a dot), "poll 20s", and refresh.
  • Lead: the derived checks, unboxed at page rank. There is no alert model; checks are re-derived on every read.
  • State region: a two-figure metric strip, then three panels: Running (two-thirds), Spend (one-third), and Headroom at full width (by runner profile, then subscription accounts).
  • About 2,776 lines, with prose.budget.test.tsx holding the word count down. Infrastructure dollars are deliberately not drawn: there is no billing integration.

What it does not answer well

  • Waiting, and why. Nothing on Overview separates PARKED (quota cooldown, checkpointed) from READY (a pool is full) from QUEUED (dependencies). All three are one "wait" triangle, and the reason needs the inspector.
  • Lifecycle at a glance. "Running" counts the four states that hold capacity, but nothing shows how much is waiting for free or what finished today.
  • Recent failures are one check row ("N failed") rather than a list you can open.
  • Cost per user task: to answer "why has mine not started" today takes Overview → Work → Agents → Waiting → a row → the inspector's attempts. That is five clicks and three reads. Every variant below answers it in one click from the waiting panel.
Alternatives considered: Lifecycle lanes and Capacity first
Layout O2

Lifecycle lanes

Idea

The state machine is the layout: three lanes (waiting, holding capacity, finished), each split by state, with a few agents shown and a "+N more" link. A strip on top carries the global pool, the binding pool, cost and accounts.

Pros

  • The platform's central idea (a lease, not a container, is what "running" means) is the shape of the page.
  • Good for a wall screen during an incident.

Risks

  • A board does not scale: at 300 queued agents the lanes are only "+N more" links.
  • There is no room for why: a parked card can carry a reason, but nothing groups them.
  • It overlaps heavily with Agents › Live and Waiting.
Layout O3

Capacity first

Idea

Four figures, then a matrix of runner profile against pool: each cell is the free slots in that pool, and the cell that binds is outlined. Underneath, the latest state changes and the recent failures.

Pros

  • It answers "which ceiling do I raise" in one look. Here, browser is stopped by class:browser and backend:GKE together, so raising one alone changes nothing.
  • Dense and exact, which suits operators.

Risks

  • Most people who land on the console submit work. They do not tune pools, and the matrix means nothing to them.
  • A "latest changes" feed implies an event log. Today it can only be built from the last task page, so it misses anything that changed twice between reads.
