Plan diagrams, proposal A: Diagrams as a section of the plan

One of three competing proposals (A to C) for diagrams in plans, each drawn on proposal D's decision page as merged (PR 2587, BGD's pick for the plan and its conversation). This one makes a diagram part of the plan document: a How it works section right after the summary holds the plan's overview diagram, each step carries its own small diagram, folded, and a diagram asked for in the thread or the chat arrives as a card with Add to plan, which makes a new revision. The switch is a card in Config › Workspace Settings, with an override on each schedule's Gates and approvers card.

What BGD asked for, 2026-10-28

“generate flow diagrams and schematics to be included in plans for the majority of coding tasks, product design or research, issue fixing, error and failure fixes and more but also not everywhere where it doesn't make sense, like fixing a line in terraform … by default on but toggleable from the automation settings”

“the interactive conversational part of the plan review … to also be able to provide on demand schematics and diagrams during the plan review process”

On-demand diagrams must work “even if the plan reviewer / approver asks this in chat with the agent that takes part in the plan review conversation, interactively in a chat section on the plan review page”.

Idea

A diagram is plan content, like a requirement or a step: the planner writes its source (a text format the console renders) into the plan, so it is inside the plan's digest and Approve covers it. The overview sits in How it works between the summary and the requirements, where a reader forms the picture before judging the parts; a step's diagram sits in its step card, folded so the document stays short. When the planner draws none, the section stays and says why in one line. A diagram drawn on request, in the anchored thread or in the chat, is not plan content until someone presses Add to plan: that makes a new revision with its own digest, and the old revision keeps its own set of diagrams.

Pros and real costs

  • Pro: what you approve is exactly what you saw. A diagram in the plan is in its digest, so a picture cannot change under an approval.
  • Pro: one place to look. The overview is always in the same section of every plan, and “no diagram, because…” sits where the diagram would be.
  • Pro: the thread and the chat stay conversations: a diagram drawn there costs a revision only when someone adds it.
  • Cost: every diagram is planner output. The overview adds about $0.10 to $0.30 to a plan (example figures; nothing measures it yet) and an on-demand drawing is an agent turn, shown with its cost.
  • Cost: Add to plan makes a revision, so an approver who adds a picture must approve again, on the new digest.
  • Cost: the console must serve a renderer (a pinned script, not a remote service), and every diagram needs a text alternative the planner writes too.
What every frame obeys
  • PICKS.md: nothing below 12px, no all-caps, a light / dark / system toggle (top of the page and every frame's panel foot), an unknown figure is a dash with its reason, never 0, state words are lower-case words.
  • Proposal D's decision page, as merged: its tokens, document, margin, revision bar, decision bar and thread. Only the diagrams, the chat section and the switch are this proposal's.
  • The canonical plan is the real run_fba2dc8f6ec9494dae2f (issue #2583, from the failure-review schedule). It carries no diagram today: every diagram drawn here is an example of what the planner would draw, inline SVG standing for what the renderer draws from the source shown in D4. Times are Wednesday 2026-10-28; the viewer is Operator, the platform owner.
  • Where the chat sits: the chat with the plan-review agent is a section of the same page, after the plan and before the foot's decision bar, reached from a Chat link in the revision bar. D's anchored thread stays in the margin, against the part it is about. Both can draw; both hand a diagram to the plan the same way.

D2

A plan where the planner drew none, and says why

A one-line change to terraform/environments/dev/dev.tfvars. The switch is on, so the planner decided, and the section says what it decided: no diagram, in one line, with its reason. The section is never removed, so every plan reads the same way, and Draw one is still there for a reviewer who wants one.

D3

On demand, in the thread: ask, wait, read, add to the plan

The reviewer asks in D's anchored thread, against the step. The planner draws; the thread shows it drawing and what it has cost so far. Its answer is a diagram card in the thread, with Add to plan. Adding it makes revision 2, with its own digest, and revision 1 keeps its two diagrams. Everything after revision 1 is an example.

D4

One diagram, large: zoom, pan, its text alternative and its source

Open large covers the page's content area; Full screen takes the whole window. Drag to pan, the wheel or the buttons to zoom. The side holds what the picture says in words (the planner writes it with the source) and the source itself.

D5

The switch: Config › Workspace Settings, and a per-schedule override

“Diagrams in plans” is a card on the workspace's Settings page, on by default. A schedule's Gates and approvers card can follow the workspace (the default), or force on or off. An issue submitted by hand follows the workspace.

D6

Honest states

A diagram box is never blank. When the picture cannot be drawn, the box says why and shows what there is: the source, and the text alternative.

D7

Phone at 390: the plan, on demand, and the chat

On the phone the plan is one column, as in D: How it works under the summary, each margin note under its part. A diagram fits the width and opens full screen on a tap. The chat is the last section, above the decision bar.

D8

The chat section: words, a diagram, and the diagram pinned or added to the plan

The chat is a conversation with the plan-review agent about the plan on the screen. It reads the plan, its revisions, its thread and the repository at the plan's base; it cannot change the plan. A question gets words; “draw …” gets a diagram. A chat diagram can be pinned (kept at the top of the chat, the plan unchanged) or added to the plan (a new revision, as in D3). Everything in the chat is an example.
