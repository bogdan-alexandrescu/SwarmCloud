Plan diagrams, proposal C: Diagrams in the conversation

One of three competing proposals for diagrams in plans, drawn on proposal D's decision page as merged (plans-d.html, PR 2587, BGD's pick on 2026-10-28 for the plan and its conversation). This one makes a diagram a message: the planner's first message in the thread carries the plan's diagrams, every diagram asked for later is a message anchored to what it explains (in the thread) or a reply in the chat, and the diagrams added to the plan are pinned in a strip above the document, inside the plan's digest.

BGD, 2026-10-28, first ask“generate flow diagrams and schematics to be included in plans for the majority of coding tasks, product design or research, issue fixing, error and failure fixes and more but also not everywhere where it doesn't make sense, like fixing a line in terraform … by default on but toggleable from the automation settings”
BGD, 2026-10-28, second ask“the interactive conversational part of the plan review … to also be able to provide on demand schematics and diagrams during the plan review process”
BGD, 2026-10-28, third additionon-demand diagrams must work “even if the plan reviewer / approver asks this in chat with the agent that takes part in the plan review conversation, interactively in a chat section on the plan review page”

Idea

A diagram belongs to the conversation that produced it. The planner opens the thread with its plan and the diagrams it judged useful (or one line saying why it drew none). A reviewer asks for another in the thread, anchored to a step, a requirement or the whole plan, and the planner answers with a diagram message; or asks the review agent in the chat, which answers in seconds. Either answer can be pinned to the plan: pinning makes a new revision whose digest covers the diagram set, so Approve always names the diagrams it approves, and the old revision keeps its own set. The chat is a column the reviewer opens beside the document; the thread stays in D's margin.

Pros

  • Every diagram says who drew it, when, from which question and at what cost: its message is its provenance.
  • Asking is the same gesture in the thread and the chat; the answer lands where the question was, next to what it explains.
  • The strip shows only what the plan carries, so “what am I approving” stays one revision, diagrams included.
  • No diagram is drawn twice: in the strip it is drawn, in its message it is named with a link to the strip.

Real costs

  • Every on-demand diagram is an agent turn: a planner answer in the thread (about $0.40 here, example) or a chat reply. Both are shown with their cost while they run, and the chat has a cap.
  • Pinning makes a revision, so a plan that gathers diagrams gathers revisions; a revision that only adds a diagram says so on its chip.
  • Two conversations on one page (the anchored thread to the planner, the chat to the review agent) need a clear line between them: only the thread can change the steps; the chat can only add a diagram.
  • Needs a diagram field in the plan, a renderer endpoint, a chat endpoint and three switches (listed at the end).

D1

A plan with its diagrams

D's decision page for run_fba2dc8f6ec9494dae2f, with one thing added above the document: the strip of diagrams pinned to this revision. The planner's first message, at the head of the margin, is where they came from: it names each one and says why it drew it. The chat is closed; its button sits in the page head.

D2

A plan where the planner drew none, and says why

The switch is on, but a diagram is the planner's call per plan: a one-line value change gets none. The strip is replaced by the planner's reason, in one line, and the reviewer can still ask for one.

D3

On demand, in the thread: ask, wait, read, add to the plan

The reviewer asks in the step's thread: show the failure path when the continuation finishes mid-check. The planner draws (its cost so far on the waiting message), answers with a diagram message anchored to the step, and Pin to the plan makes revision 2. Revision 1 keeps its 2 diagrams. Every message, cost and digest after revision 1 is an example.

D4

One diagram opened large

Any diagram (a strip tab, a thread message, a chat reply) opens over the page: zoom and pan, full screen, its text alternative, and its source, which is what the plan stores and what the digest covers.

D5

The switch: a workspace default, per schedule, per issue submission

On by default, as BGD asked. The workspace default lives in Config › Workspace Settings; a schedule overrides it on its Gates and approvers card; an issue submitted by hand overrides it on Submit › From a GitHub issue. On means the planner may draw (and decides per plan) and anyone reviewing may ask; off means no diagrams and no way to ask.

D7

Phone at 390: the plan, a diagram asked for, the chat

On the phone the strip draws each diagram in a tall layout of the same source (the renderer is asked for top-to-bottom at this width), so the page never scrolls sideways. A sequence diagram has no tall layout: in the thread it scrolls inside its own box, and Open large shows it whole. The thread sits under each part, as in D's P7. The chat opens as a sheet over the plan.

D8

The chat section: words, a diagram, and pinning it

The chat is a column on the right of the decision page, opened from the page head; while it is open the margin's reasons and thread fold under each part of the document, as on the phone. The reviewer talks with the review agent, which reads the plan on screen and the repository at the plan's base, writes nothing, and answers in words or with a diagram. It cannot change a step: that is the thread's job. It can offer a diagram for pinning, which makes a revision exactly as in D3. Revision 2 here is D3's; every message and cost is an example.
