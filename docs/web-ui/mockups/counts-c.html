Platform counts over time, proposal C: combined ledger with drill-down

BGD asked on 2026-10-23 for Admin › Platform counts to show agents over time "in a nice set of visualizations like graphs and charts". Five lanes draw five distinct proposals; he picks one. This page is proposal C only: one large combined chart, and a drill-down under it for the bucket you click. Nothing here is built.

All data is example data: the user is Operator, the workspaces are eng, smoke and u-operator, the host is swarm.example.com, today is Friday 23 October 2026, and every number is invented. Units: agents are counted once, on the day they were submitted; retries are their attempts after the first; run hours are summed attempt run time; tokens are in millions (M) or billions (B); cost is US dollars at list price.

CCombined ledger with drill-down

The page keeps everything it has today, in the same order: the head's priced Run the count · 24 reads for the live state counts, the two scope cards, and Platform ticks. A new Over time card sits between the scope cards and the ticks, with its own priced Run.

Idea

One large chart answers "how much ran, and at what cost" for every bucket at once. Bars are agents, with retries as a lighter segment on top, read on the left axis. A line of diamonds is the estimated cost, read on a second axis on the right. Numbered notes mark the unusual buckets, and a list under the chart explains each one. Clicking a bar opens that bucket below the chart. There, its agents are ranked by agent type, model, workspace or pool account as horizontal bars, next to its own totals and a token-composition bar.

Pros

  • Volume and cost are read together, bucket by bucket. When the line leaves the bars, cost per agent has moved, and a note says so in words.
  • The main chart carries only two series, and they differ in shape (bar, diamond on a line) as well as hue. It never becomes a stacked rainbow of agent types.
  • A breakdown appears only where it is asked for, at full width, with every figure and its coverage.
  • Reads stay small. The span is one priced run. A bucket's split reads only that bucket's sealed days (one read per workspace per day: 3 × 7 = 21 reads for a week, 3 × 30 = 90 for a 30-day month), and its button says so before you press it.

Risks

  • Two value axes. The two scales are independent, so where the line crosses a bar means nothing. A reader may still read meaning into it. Mitigations: each axis is titled with its own mark, the cost axis and its ticks are drawn in the cost hue, and the two scales share no gridline. If BGD dislikes it, the fallback is the cost line in its own lane under the bars, on the same time axis.
  • A trend per agent type over time is not shown. You compare types one bucket at a time, by clicking. Other proposals draw that trend.
  • A bucket's split is a second read. usage.groups (COUNTS-ROLLUP, PR 2154) is span-level, so the split comes from a request bounded to that bucket.
  • The token bar uses all four series hues. Teal (--series-2) fails the palette validator's chroma floor, and teal next to blue fails its normal-vision floor. Teal is therefore placed last, next to violet, with 2px gaps, and every kind is named with its value.

F6Interaction: the readout, and picking a breakdown

Esc goes back to the whole span. Nothing is read on hover or focus, and changing the bucket or the breakdown never reads on its own: the press does.

F7Phone: how the charts reflow at 390

The page never scrolls sideways at 390. Each part follows a rule:

  1. The combined chart is drawn for 342 units, not scaled down. It has its own geometry: narrower margins, bars at two-thirds of the step, every sixth week or third month labelled, and the right axis titled "$ est.". Every label stays at 12px, so the build authors a wide and a narrow drawing, as the Timeline ledger does (charts/README.md, "Drawn three times, not scaled").
  2. The legend becomes a list, one mark per line, each still naming its axis.
  3. The summary strip becomes two by two. Each figure keeps its coverage line.
  4. A tap replaces hover. The bucket is banded, and its readout rises as a sheet with one button to open it.
  5. The drill-down stacks. First the bucket's totals, then its token bar with its four rows, then the breakdown picker as a select, then the ranked rows. Each row puts its name and agent count on one line, the bar at full width under them, and run hours, tokens and cost on a third line.
  6. The live state cards stack, one per scope, exactly as they do today.
