Plans, proposal A: Proposals inbox and side sheet

One of four competing proposals (A to D) for BGD's redesign of how the console shows what automatic runs propose, and how a plan is judged and approved. Each draws the same frames (P1 to P7). This one is built for fast triage: everything lands in one inbox, and one plan opens in a side sheet over it, with the decision bar always at the sheet's foot.

Idea

Approvals grows into the one inbox for everything an automatic run proposes: the issue sweep's plans, the failure-review schedule's plans, observer proposals to file as issues, a plan for an issue submitted by hand, and the merges those plans end in. It is grouped by what waits on a person first (oldest first, with the time to expiry), then what was decided, then what ended without a decision, and it filters by source and by state. One plan opens in a right side sheet over the list, as a schedule does in the picked Automate A1/A2, read top to bottom in the order a decision needs: why it exists, what it changes, what it collides with, what it costs, what it touches that is risky, its digest. A sticky Approve and run · Edit plan · Reject bar sits at the sheet's foot, so the next row is one click away and the decision never scrolls out of reach.

Why Approvals, not a new tab

  • The inbox already projects a PLANNED run as kind plan or hold (approvals.project_run) beside merge, proposal and the rest, and an approve there calls the run's own _approve with the digest shown. A separate Proposals tab would list the same items a second time.
  • A plan's merge is the same flow's last decision; keeping it in the same list means a person follows one proposal from plan to merge without changing pages.
  • Work › Runs keeps every run. A PLANNED run's row there says "waiting in Approvals" and opens this same sheet, so there is one decision surface, not two.

How it differs from the console today

  • Today a plan is judged on the run's page in Work › Runs (RunSteps.tsx), and the inbox row only links there. Here the plan is read and decided in the sheet, over the inbox.
  • Today the inbox shows waiting items and those decided as records; a projected plan disappears once decided. Here the inbox also lists plans auto-approved by a gate, rejected, expired and superseded (a new read, listed at the end).
  • Today the plan view shows steps, overlaps, requirements and the digest. Here it adds the source (schedule, firing, failure), a risk-path check per category, the expected cost, and each step's runner and model.
  • Today the gates are read on each schedule's page. Here a Sources view of the inbox lists every source that proposes, its plan and merge gates, its spend, and how much it proposed and how much was approved.

Pros and risks

  • Pro: triage at speed: the list stays in view, the decision bar never moves, and j/k step to the next row with the sheet open.
  • Pro: no new section in the nav; the built address /automate/approvals/<id> opens the sheet.
  • Risk: a long plan (8 steps, many overlaps) is a long sheet; it folds prompts and overlaps past the first, and the bar stays put, but it is still a narrow column at 760 px.
  • Risk: the inbox mixes plans, proposals and merges; the source and kind filters carry that, and a person who only wants plans has to filter.
What every frame obeys
  • PICKS.md: nothing below 12px, no all-caps, no letter-spacing, a light / dark / system toggle (top of this page and in every frame's panel foot), an unknown figure is a dash with its reason, never 0. State words are lower case (waiting, approved), never the API's enums.
  • Style is Automate A's, which is Config E's and Subscription accounts A's: the four plan proposals differ in layout and flow, not in style.
  • The canonical plan is real: run run_fba2dc8f6ec9494dae2f, from the failure-review schedule sch_54e9828e6772, firing sch_54e9828e6772:29885760, issue bogdan-alexandrescu/SwarmCloud#2583. Its words are the plan's own, cut where the excerpt ends; what this page does not quote is folded or a dash, never made up. P1

Where proposals land: Automate › Approvals

Eleven items from every source. Waiting first, oldest first, each with the time to its expiry and who may decide; then decided in the last 7 days (auto-approved by a gate, approved, rejected, each with its reason); then ended without a decision (expired, superseded).

P2

One plan to decide: the plan for #2583

The canonical plan opens in the side sheet over the inbox. It reads in the order a decision needs: why it exists, what it says, what it requires, what it overlaps, its steps, what it costs, what it touches that is risky, its digest. The decision bar is sticky at the sheet's foot.

P3

Editing a plan

Edit plan turns the same sheet into its editor, in place. Every change is drawn against the revision it replaces; Save sends the digest it opened on, so two editors cannot overwrite each other, and makes a new revision with a new digest, which is then approved like any other.

P4

After approval: the plan traced to what it became

The same sheet, reopened from the inbox's Decided group or from Work › Runs, now leads with the trace: the revision approved, each step's task and its state, the pull request, CI and its fix rounds, the review's verdict and requirements, the merge, and the keyword the pull request carries. Everything past the approval is example: it is what #2583's plan would become.

The keyword is swarm-api's, never an agent's: only a complete verdict list with every requirement met sets requirements_met and writes Closes #N; anything else writes part of #N naming what is left (docs/issue-runs.md).

P5

The automatic sources and their gates

The inbox's second view, Sources: every schedule or observer that proposes, and the hand-submitted path, with the gates each has. Only gates that exist are drawn: the schedule's gate.plan (approve or auto), gate.merge (approve, or auto only through the audited switch, POST /v1/schedules/{id}:merge-mode), gate.approvers and gate.approval_ttl_hours; for a hand-submitted run, its own plan_approval and auto_merge.

P6

Empty and honest states

What the inbox says when there is nothing, when a source is paused, when a read failed, and when this deployment does not serve plans. A failed read is a dash with its reason, never 0.

P7

Phone at 390

On a phone the inbox's rows become cards and the sheet becomes the page, with a back link to the inbox; the decision bar stays sticky at the foot of the screen.
