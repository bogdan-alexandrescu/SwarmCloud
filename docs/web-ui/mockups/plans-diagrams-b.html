Plan diagrams, proposal B: Diagram canvas beside the plan

One of three competing proposals for diagrams in plans, drawn on proposal D's decision page as merged (plans-d.html, PR 2587, BGD's pick on 2026-10-28 for the plan and its conversation). This one gives the diagrams their own place: D's margin gains a sticky canvas with tabs (Overview, one per step, Asked). Scrolling the document puts the part you are reading on the canvas; clicking a node scrolls the document to the step it names. Drawing on demand is a Draw… composer on the canvas, a request in the anchored thread, or a line in the chat with the plan-review agent, which sits under the canvas in the same rail.

What BGD asked for, 2026-10-28
  • (1) “generate flow diagrams and schematics to be included in plans for the majority of coding tasks, product design or research, issue fixing, error and failure fixes and more but also not everywhere where it doesn't make sense, like fixing a line in terraform … by default on but toggleable from the automation settings”
  • (2) “the interactive conversational part of the plan review … to also be able to provide on demand schematics and diagrams during the plan review process”
  • (3) on-demand diagrams must work “even if the plan reviewer / approver asks this in chat with the agent that takes part in the plan review conversation, interactively in a chat section on the plan review page”

Idea

A plan's diagrams are a set beside the document, not figures inside it. The canvas is sticky in D's margin: it shows the diagram for the part you are reading (the overview while you read why, summary and requirements; step 1's while you read step 1), and its tabs let you pick any other. Nodes are links: a check the plan adds opens the step that adds it. Under the canvas the margin keeps D's reasons and thread for the part in view, and under that the chat: a free conversation with the plan-review agent. A diagram asked for anywhere (Draw…, the thread, the chat) lands on the canvas's Asked tab first; Add to plan makes it part of the plan as a new revision with its own digest, and the old revision keeps its diagram set.

Pros

  • The plan document reads exactly as in D; the diagrams never push its text apart, so a plan with no diagram looks no different.
  • One diagram is always in view beside the step it explains, with no scrolling back to a figure.
  • Every way of asking (Draw…, the thread, the chat) answers in one place, the Asked tab, so a diagram is never lost in a long thread.
  • Presets (flow, sequence, data, failure path) turn “draw something” into a short, cheap request.

Real costs

  • The rail grows from D's 330px to 452px, so the document column narrows by about 120px at 1440.
  • Scroll-linked highlighting is new front-end work (an intersection observer per plan part) and has to stay calm: the canvas changes only when a part fills most of the view.
  • Three ways to ask is more to explain than one; each answer is an agent run and shows its cost, and the chat has a cap per plan.
  • On the phone there is no room for a sticky rail: the canvas becomes a card at the top and the chat a sheet (D7).
  • Needs new API: diagrams on the plan (inside its digest), a draw endpoint, the chat, and a setting with a per-schedule override (listed at the end).
What every frame obeys
  • PICKS.md: nothing below 12px, no all-caps, a light / dark / system toggle (top of the page and every frame's panel foot), an unknown figure is a dash with its reason, never 0, and state words are lower-case words, never the API's enums.
  • D's tokens, decision page, revision bar, decision bar and margin, copied whole. Automate's tabs are the built Schedules and Approvals, Pipeline (BGD's PL1 pick, proposal C, not built yet) and Settings, which this proposal adds.

D2

A plan where the planner drew none, and says why

The switch is on, but the planner judged a diagram would explain nothing. The canvas does not disappear (the page would jump between plans); it shrinks to one line with the planner's reason, and Draw… is still there if you disagree.

D3

On demand: ask in the thread, wait, read, add it to the plan

The reviewer asks the planner for a diagram in the anchored thread on step 1. The answer lands on the canvas's Asked tab and as a reply in the thread; Add to plan makes it part of the plan as revision 2. Every message, cost and digest after revision 1 is an example.

D4

One diagram opened large

Open large (or a double-click on the canvas) fills the content area: zoom and pan, the text alternative beside it, and the source it was drawn from. Esc or Back returns to the page at the same scroll position.

D5

The switch: Automate › Settings, and a per-schedule override

A new Settings tab under Automate holds the workspace default for diagrams in plans: on unless someone turns it off. Each schedule that plans can follow it, or override it on or off. One switch covers both kinds: off means the planner draws none and nobody can ask for one (no Draw…, no draw in the thread or chat).

D7

Phone at 390: the plan, an ask, and the chat

No sticky rail: the canvas is a card at the top of the plan with the same tabs, each step carries a Diagram chip that opens its tab, and the chat is a sheet opened from the foot. While a draw asked in a thread runs, the canvas docks under that thread (D7b), so the ask and its progress share one screen.

D8

The chat with the plan-review agent

The chat sits at the foot of the rail, under the canvas, on every plan. It is a conversation with the plan-review agent about the plan on the screen: it answers in words, and when asked to draw it draws. Its diagrams land on the Asked tab like any other; Pin to plan is the chat's Add to plan. It never changes the plan by itself, and it is not the thread: the thread is anchored to a part and goes to the planner; the chat is free and goes to the review agent. Every message, cost and digest below is an example, and a separate one from D3's: here nothing was asked in the thread, so pinning makes revision 2.
