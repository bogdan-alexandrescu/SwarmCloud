Automate: proposal D, Config E's sections

One of four competing proposals (A to D) for BGD's redesign of the console's Automate section. BGD picks frames across the four on the picks page, so every proposal draws the same frames under the same ids, A1 to A7. This one draws Automate the way Config E draws Config: a setup strip, then sections, then detail pages that open with a lead and stack their cards. Every desktop frame is drawn at 1440 wide (or this page's width when the window is narrower) with the console's nav open; the phone frames are drawn 390 wide. The theme control at the top repaints the page and every frame light, dark or as the system says, and so does the control in each frame's panel foot.

What every frame obeys
  • PICKS.md: nothing below 12px, no all-caps, no letter-spacing, a light / dark / system toggle in the panel's user foot, and honesty: an unknown figure is a dash with its reason, never 0. State words are lower-case words ("waiting on you", "approved", "rejected"), never the API's enums.
  • The V2 Sky spine and the console's tokens, light and dark, taken from Config E (config-e.html, #2157) and Subscription accounts A (accounts-a.html, #2222): the same lead, cards, tables, chips, buttons, wizard sheet and phone rows-as-cards. Nothing here is a new visual language.
  • Example data. The viewer is Operator, the owner of the group workspace Example Team and a platform admin; A5f is drawn for Lee, a member. The approval in A5 is the real apr_31f74dc3dc84349da9b83697 with its real fields. Times are UTC and "now" is Monday 2026-10-26 09:20Z.

Idea

Automate is led by what it is waiting for. A setup strip runs across the top: schedule types enabled → approvers set → budget tripwires set → merge App set, each step read, never ticked by hand. Under it the landing has three sections in a fixed order: Needs you (approvals waiting and schedules that are stuck), Schedules (cards grouped by type, each with its next firing and last outcome) and Recent firings. One schedule and one approval are Config E detail pages: a header, one lead sentence with its button, then a stack of cards. Making or editing a schedule is Config E's six-step wizard in a side sheet.

How it differs from the console today

  • Today Schedules is one table (Name · type, Repository, When, Next run, Last run, Spend today, Approvals, State) and Approvals is a split list beside a detail. Here the table becomes cards grouped by type, under the two things a table cannot say first: what waits on you, and what is stuck.
  • Automate keeps its two pages, Schedules and Approvals (SD1). The landing's Needs you shows the waiting approvals, but the full inbox, its kind filter and its decided history stay a page of their own, because they do not fit in a section.
  • ScheduleDetail's five tabs (History, Budget, Gate, Settings, Audit) become one stack of cards with a lead; ScheduleEdit's one long form becomes six steps; one approval gets a page of its own rather than the right half of the inbox.

Risks

  • The strip costs about 56px on the landing even when done; it folds to one line (A6d), but it is still there.
  • Two of the strip's steps are read across schedules (approvers, tripwires) and two need reads that do not exist: whether the merge App is set, and nothing serves "types enabled per workspace" (see New fields).
  • Cards hold fewer schedules on one screen than the table: about nine at 1440 against the table's twenty. A workspace with forty schedules wants the table back; the Schedules heading keeps a Cards / Table switch for that.
  • Recent firings across every schedule needs one tenant-wide read; today it is one read per schedule.

New fields this proposal needs

Every other field a frame draws is served today by apps/swarm-api: the schedule (state, pause, cron, timezone, words, next_run_at, last_firing, consecutive_failures, spend_today, budget, budget_use, gate, pending_approvals, tier), its firings (slot, trigger, state, outcome, skip, work, cost, approval_id), the preview (words, next, min_gap_minutes, refusal), the schedule types (available, disabled_reason, budget_caps, min_interval_minutes, gate_notes), and the approval (kind, summary, state, subject, digest, approvers, hold, requested_at, expires_at, decided_by_name, decided_at, reason, and the merge block's pull_request.check_counts, head_sha, merged_at).

Open questions, and what this proposal decided

  • "Denied" or "rejected". The brief says denied; the API's state is rejected, its route :reject, and the built console says Reject… and Rejected. The frames draw "rejected" so as not to rename a built word; A5c is labelled denied so the picks line up.
  • The merge App step. Drawn as the strip's fourth step, as the brief asks, and as the next step in A1. If BGD agrees the App is the platform's CI identity rather than a workspace's, the step shows only to a platform admin and a workspace's strip has three.
  • "Active" for enabled. The cards say active, paused, paused by its spend tripwire (auto_paused with RUN_OVER_BUDGET), waiting on you (enabled with a pending approval), failing (enabled with failures in a row) and never fired: the state a person acts on, not the stored enum. Disabled (a platform admin's) would read "turned off by a platform admin".
  • Two pages, kept. Automate keeps Schedules and Approvals: the landing summarises approvals, but the inbox's filter and history are a page.
